# Teacher guide: Music Theory Basics

**Status:** Demo — not approved for classroom use yet.

## Outcomes
Students explain that pitch is how high or low a sound is, that faster vibrations make higher pitches, and that loudness is something different. They name notes on the treble staff (middle C to G5) and find them on a keyboard, including both names for a black key. They know how many beats whole, dotted half, half, quarter, and eighth notes and rests last in 4/4 time, build measures of exactly 4 beats, and read a short melody.

## Before class
- Press **Test sound** and set the volume on the projector or Chromebooks. Headphones help when students work on their own. Press **M** (or the Sound button) to mute at any time; every activity still works by lighting up notes, keys, and counts.
- Try the piano three ways: click or tap (slide a finger across the keys for a glissando), the computer keyboard (click the piano, then A S D F G H J K for white keys and W E T Y U for black keys), and the arrow keys with Enter.
- Decide whether to show **Letter names on the keys**. Turn them off once students can find C by looking for the pair of black keys.
- Nothing is saved, and no student information is collected. Scores disappear when the page closes. Sound is made in the browser; no files or websites are loaded.

## Facilitation steps
**Pitch explorer (10 minutes)**
1. Play a very low and a very high note. Ask: "What is different about these two sounds?" Then open **High and low** and press **Hear it**.
2. Open **Loud is not high**. Have students describe each sound before you reveal which is which. Point to the wave: taller means louder, closer together means higher.
3. Use **Seven letters**, **Octaves**, and **Sharps and flats** in that order. Have students predict the name of a key before you press it.
4. Finish with **Lines and spaces** and **Middle C**. Have students invent their own E-G-B-D-F sentence.

**Rhythm builder (10 minutes)**
1. Start in **Free build** with **Click the beat** on. Clap the beat together, then add one note at a time and play it.
2. Try to add a half note when only 1 beat is left so the class sees the message. Ask: "What could fit instead?"
3. Turn on **Show fractions of a measure** for a math connection: two eighths make a quarter, two quarters make a half.
4. **Echo challenge:** play the rhythm, have the class clap it back, then build it together and press **Check**.

**Read a song (10 minutes)**
1. Pick **Hot Cross Buns** (3 notes) or **Mary Had a Little Lamb** (4 notes). Step through 1 to 6 and pause on each **Discuss** prompt.
2. On step 7, play the song on **Slow** while students point to each note.
3. On ★ **Your turn**, students play the song by reading it. Turn on **Light up the next key** for students who need it. Try **Twinkle** and **Ode to Joy** next.

**Name that note (5 to 10 minutes)**
1. Start with **Read the note**, then **How long?**, then **Higher or lower?**. **Mix** works well as a review.
2. On a whiteboard, students can take turns. Individually, have them replay **Practice the ones I missed**.

## Discussion prompts
- Can you sing a high note quietly? A low note loudly? What does that tell you about pitch and loudness?
- Why do you think music uses only seven letter names, then starts over?
- Why does every measure in 4/4 hold exactly 4 beats? What would go wrong if measures had different numbers of beats?
- A rest is silence. Why does music need to write silence down?
- Where does Mary Had a Little Lamb go up? Where does it go down? How can you see that on the staff?

## Misconceptions and how to address them
- **Higher means louder:** **Loud is not high** plays all four combinations, and the wave shows height vs. spacing. In **Higher or lower?**, the higher note is sometimes quieter on purpose, and the feedback says so.
- **Up on the page or keyboard doesn't match up in sound:** Every note played in the explorer appears on the staff at the same moment; the **High and low** demo climbs both.
- **The letters keep going (H, I, J):** **Seven letters** plays C to C, and the explorer shows the octave number change.
- **Every pair of white keys is a whole step:** **Sharps and flats** points out E–F and B–C, and "Compared with" counts half steps for any two keys.
- **A black key has one name:** Black keys always show both names, and the ♯/♭ switch changes how the staff writes them.
- **All notes are one beat, or length depends on pitch:** The rhythm builder uses one pitch for every note so only length changes; **How long?** feedback explains hollow vs. filled, stems, flags, and dots.
- **Rests don't count:** Rests take up space in the beat bar and the counts keep going during them; feedback reminds students rests are counted like notes.
- **Half rest vs. whole rest:** Feedback uses "hat" (sits on the middle line, half rest) and "hole" (hangs from the 4th line, whole rest).
- **A measure can hold any number of beats:** The builder refuses notes that would overflow a measure and explains why.

## Assessment / expected responses
- Pitch: "Faster vibrations make a higher sound. Loudness is how big the vibrations are."
- Treble lines from the bottom are E, G, B, D, F; spaces are F, A, C, E. Middle C is on a ledger line below the staff.
- The black key between F and G is F♯ or G♭.
- Beats in 4/4: whole 4, dotted half 3, half 2, quarter 1, eighth ½; whole rest 4, half rest 2, quarter rest 1.
- Hot Cross Buns: E D C, E D C, C C C C D D D D, E D C. Mary Had a Little Lamb starts E D C D E E E.
- Strong explanation of a dotted note: "The dot adds half of the note's length, so a dotted half is 2 + 1 = 3 beats."

## Accommodations and extension ideas
- Support: Keep letter names on, use **Slow** in songs, turn on **Light up the next key**, and start with Hot Cross Buns. The question can be read aloud with the 🔊 button in the game.
- Support: Keyboard and switch users can do everything with Tab, arrow keys, Enter, Space, and Backspace. Students who are deaf or hard of hearing can use the lesson muted; the listening questions turn into reading questions.
- Challenge: Turn letter names off. Use **Mix** in the game. In the rhythm builder, write a rhythm on paper first, then build it and check it by ear.
- Challenge: Use the explorer to work out a different tune by ear (for example, "Row, Row, Row Your Boat" starts C C C D E).
- Extension (math): Use fractions mode to show that ½ + ¼ + ⅛ + ⅛ = 1 measure. How many different ways can students fill one measure?
- Extension (science): Connect to sound as vibration: rubber bands, rulers on a desk edge, or water glasses. Shorter or tighter makes higher.

## Simplifications / limitations
- Sounds are simple computer tones, not a recorded piano. The vibration picture is a plain sine wave; real instruments make more complex waves.
- Only the treble clef and 4/4 time are used. Bass clef, key signatures, other meters, ties, and sixteenth notes are left out.
- Songs are written in C and simplified. Ode to Joy is in a common beginner version, and Twinkle, Twinkle is only its first line.
- **Your turn** checks the order of notes, not rhythm or timing.
- The piano on phones shows one octave at a time. Sliding a finger across the keys means a scroll can't start on the piano itself.
- Audio needs a browser with the Web Audio API (all current Chromebook, Windows, Mac, iPad, and Android browsers). Without it, the lesson says so and still works visually. On iPhones and iPads, the ring/silent switch may mute web audio.
