# Lesson brief: Music Theory Basics

- Subject / grade: Music (general music) / Grade 5. This is the first music lesson in Learning Lab, so it adds a new "Music" subject to the home page. Confirm placement with the music teacher; much of this is often introduced in grades 3 to 5.
- Student learning objective (observable): The student (1) explains that pitch is how high or low a sound is and that faster vibration makes a higher pitch, and that loudness is a different property; (2) names notes on the treble staff from middle C to G5 and finds them on a piano keyboard, including the two names of a black key; (3) states how many beats a whole, dotted half, half, quarter, and eighth note (and whole, half, and quarter rests) last in 4/4 time, and builds measures that hold exactly 4 beats; (4) reads and plays a short melody from notation.
- Topic / motivating question: How can marks on paper tell a musician exactly which sounds to make, and for how long?
- Standards (jurisdiction + version/year + exact code + URL; or TBD): TBD. Oklahoma adopted new Academic Standards for Fine Arts in 2023 (https://legacy.sde.ok.gov/sites/default/files/2023%20OAS%20FA%20FINAL_0.pdf). The teacher should check which grade 5 general music standards this supports before any codes are attached. Don't assume National Core Arts Standards codes carry over.
- Lesson type: Interactive (browser), four activities in one lesson, plus a shared sound bar:
  0. **Sound bar:** Sound on/off (also the M key), volume, and a test chime. Sound is made in the browser with the Web Audio API. Nothing plays until a student presses something. When muted, every activity still works by lighting up notes, keys, and counts.
  1. **Pitch explorer:** a two-octave piano (C4 to C6; one octave at a time with an octave switch on phones). Every key played shows the note on a treble staff, its name (both names for black keys), where it sits on the staff, its frequency, a picture of its vibration, and how it compares with the last note (half steps, octaves). Seven "big ideas" (High and low, Seven letters, Octaves, Loud is not high, Sharps and flats, Lines and spaces, Middle C) each have a short explanation, a discussion question, and a **Hear it** demo that lights up the piano and staff as it plays.
  2. **Rhythm builder:** two measures of 4/4 on a one-line rhythm staff with a beat bar and "1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &" counting underneath. Students add notes and rests from a palette. A note that doesn't fit gets an explanation of how many beats are left and what would fit. Play uses a steady beat click, adjustable tempo (50 to 150 beats per minute), and an optional repeat. "Show fractions of a measure" relabels everything as ½, ¼, ⅛ for a math connection. **Echo challenge:** six rhythms to copy by ear, with feedback that names the first measure and beat that differs, and a **Show me** after two misses. When sound is off, the target rhythm is written out instead.
  3. **Read a song:** four public-domain tunes (Hot Cross Buns, Mary Had a Little Lamb, Twinkle Twinkle first line, Ode to Joy) shown in notation. Seven guided steps highlight the staff, clef, time signature, bar lines, note shapes, and note positions, then Listen and follow (each note lights up as it sounds; medium or slow). ★ **Your turn:** play the song on the piano by reading it. Wrong keys get feedback such as "one step higher; it is on the 1st line." After two misses on a note, the right key glows.
  4. **Name that note:** rounds of 8 questions of three kinds (or mixed): **Read the note** (letter buttons or A to G keys), **How long?** (beats for a note or rest), and **Higher or lower?** (listen to two notes; sometimes the higher note is quieter on purpose). Feedback targets common mix-ups (one step off, line vs. space, half vs. whole note or rest, dots, flags, loud vs. high). Round ends with stars, a review list, and **Practice the ones I missed**.
- Target duration: About 35 minutes for all four; each activity also works alone in 8 to 15 minutes.
- Classroom setting: Projector or interactive whiteboard for whole-class demos, or individual Chromebooks and tablets (headphones recommended).
- Prior knowledge: Experience singing and clapping simple songs; fractions ½ and ¼ help with the rhythm fractions option.
- Key misconceptions: see `lesson.json`. The biggest: higher means louder; the letters go past G; note length depends on pitch; rests don't count; E–F and B–C are whole steps.
- Student actions and feedback: Play keys by mouse, touch (with sliding), computer keyboard, or arrow keys; build and play rhythms; copy rhythms by ear; play songs from notation; answer reading, rhythm, and listening questions with mistake-specific feedback.
- Formative assessment / evidence of learning: Echo challenge checks, song play-through slips, game score and missed-item list (none of it is stored), and discussion prompts in the explorer and song steps.
- Accessibility / device constraints: Every key, palette item, and answer is a real button. The piano is one Tab stop with arrow keys, Home/End, and Enter/Space; letter keys play notes when it is focused. Notes on the rhythm staff can be selected with Tab and Enter; Backspace/Delete removes. Live regions announce notes and feedback. Everything that makes a sound also lights up, so the lesson works muted or for students who are deaf or hard of hearing (the Higher or lower questions switch to reading when sound is off). Animations stop under reduced motion. On phones, the piano shows one octave at a time and the line being played is repeated just above the piano.
- Reliable educational sources: Open Music Theory, Version 2 (open textbook) for clefs and note names; OpenStax College Physics 17.6 for pitch, frequency, and the 20 to 20,000 Hz hearing range. All notation is drawn with original SVG; no fonts, images, or question banks are copied. Songs are public domain melodies arranged here.
- Known simplifications and limitations: see `lesson.json` `modelLimitations`.
- Teacher sign-off needed: Yes. Confirm grade placement, vocabulary (half step, ledger line, octave numbers like C4), the mnemonic choice, the song versions, and the facts listed in the teacher guide.
