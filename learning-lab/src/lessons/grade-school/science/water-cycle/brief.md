# Lesson brief: The Water Cycle

- Subject / grade: Science / Grade 5
- Student learning objective (observable): The student names and describes evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and collection (runoff and groundwater), and traces at least two different paths a water drop could take.
- Topic / motivating question: Where does a raindrop come from, and where can it go next?
- Standards (jurisdiction + version/year + exact code + URL; or TBD): TBD. Verify against the Oklahoma Academic Standards for Science at https://oklahoma.gov/education/services/standards-learning/oklahoma-academic-standards.html with the teacher. Don't assume NGSS codes carry over.
- Lesson type: Interactive (browser) with a simple drawn scene that highlights one process per stage.
- Target duration: 15 minutes.
- Classroom setting: Projector for a whole-class walkthrough, or individual Chromebooks.
- Prior knowledge: Solids, liquids, and gases; heating and cooling.
- Key misconceptions:
  - The water cycle is a fixed four-step loop that every drop follows in order.
  - Clouds are made of water vapor (they are tiny liquid droplets and/or ice crystals; water vapor is invisible).
  - Rain only collects on the surface; students often leave out groundwater.
- Student actions and feedback: Students step through the stages. Each stage highlights and animates its process in the scene, gives a short explanation, and asks a discussion question.
- Formative assessment / evidence of learning: Students answer the discussion prompts and describe two different paths a drop could take (for example, ocean → cloud → hillside → groundwater → ocean).
- Accessibility / device constraints: Keyboard-operable buttons; a screen-reader description of the scene for each stage; animation stops when reduced motion is on; the in-scene labels are hidden on narrow screens, where the stage name appears right below the picture.
- Reliable educational sources: TBD. Teacher to confirm with the district's adopted science materials. A good general reference is the USGS Water Science School page on the water cycle (https://www.usgs.gov/special-topics/water-science-school/science/water-cycle).
- Known simplifications and limitations: Not to scale. Shows only one route. Transpiration, snowpack, glaciers, and lakes are mentioned in the text but not drawn. Water vapor is drawn as wavy lines even though it is invisible.
- Teacher sign-off needed: Yes.
