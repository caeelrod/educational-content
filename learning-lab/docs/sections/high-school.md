# High School section

Id: `high-school` · Lessons: `src/lessons/high-school/` · Curriculum: `curriculum/high-school/`

## Audience and level
Students in grades 9 to 12 taking their first course in a subject: Physical Science, Biology, Chemistry, Physics, Algebra, Geometry, Economics, and similar. Each lesson names its course and grade band in its brief and states its prerequisites on its first screen.

Lessons sit between Grade School and College. They use the formal vocabulary, units, and equations of a high-school course, but assume nothing beyond the course's usual prerequisites. Each should give students a way to test an idea against evidence (a simulation, a data set, a model) before stating the rule.

## Language level
Grade 9 to 10 reading level in the main path, so the lesson works for the youngest students in the course. Use the technical terms a high-school textbook uses, define each one the first time it appears, and pair equations with a plain-language reading. Keep optional deeper material in clearly marked extensions.

## Review authority
A high-school teacher of the lesson's subject. A lesson stays `demo` until the teacher approves its accuracy, level, and usage context and the approval is recorded in its `teacher-guide.md` with reviewer and date.

## Curriculum sources
- Oklahoma Academic Standards (the same jurisdiction as Grade School): https://oklahoma.gov/education/services/standards-learning/oklahoma-academic-standards.html. The adopted text is also published in the Oklahoma Administrative Code, Title 210, Chapter 15 (for example science at https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/oklahoma/OAC-210-15-3-82 for Physics).
- Confirm the version and identifier with the teacher before listing a code in `lesson.json` or `registry.ts`. Until then, record it as a candidate in the lesson's brief and in `curriculum/high-school/scope-and-sequence.md`. Don't treat NGSS codes as interchangeable with Oklahoma's.
- Cite openly licensed references for content, such as OpenStax *Physics* (https://openstax.org/details/books/physics), *Biology 2e*, or *Chemistry 2e*, naming the chapter and section checked.

## Setting and devices
Individual or paired work on school Chromebooks, tablets, and phones; whole-class use on a projector. School networks often filter sites or block external assets, so lessons must not depend on them.

## Extra safety and privacy rules
Most learners are minors. On top of the shared policies:
- Suggested labs or demonstrations must suit a school lab with teacher supervision and follow the school's safety rules; never give instructions for hazardous procedures.
- Never collect anything that could identify a student, even optionally.
