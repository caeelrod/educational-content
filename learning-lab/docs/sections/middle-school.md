# Middle School section

Id: `middle-school` · Lessons: `src/lessons/middle-school/` · Curriculum: `curriculum/middle-school/`

## Audience and level
Students in grades 6 to 8. Lessons sit between the hands-on Grade School activities and the abstract College material: students still learn best by doing, but they are ready to work with evidence, models, variables, and reasoning, and to explain their thinking in writing. Each lesson's brief names the grade it was written for and asks the teacher to confirm placement.

## Language level
Plain language at a middle-school reading level. Use the subject's real vocabulary (for example claim, ratio, or tectonic plate), because students will meet it in class and on tests, but define each term where it first appears. Keep sentences short and put optional deeper material in clearly marked extensions.

## Review authority
A middle-school teacher of the lesson's subject. A lesson stays `demo` until the teacher approves its accuracy, age appropriateness, and usage context and the approval is recorded in its `teacher-guide.md` with reviewer and date.

## Curriculum sources
- Oklahoma Academic Standards: https://oklahoma.gov/education/services/standards-learning/oklahoma-academic-standards.html
- Teachers in other states should use their own state's adopted standards. Don't treat Common Core or NGSS as automatically interchangeable with a state's adopted standards, and verify when each version applies before attaching codes.
- Record the confirmed pacing guide in `curriculum/middle-school/scope-and-sequence.md`. Leave `standards` empty until a teacher confirms them.

## Setting and devices
Whole-class use on a projector or interactive whiteboard, and individual or partner work on school Chromebooks, tablets, and phones. School networks often filter sites or block external assets, so lessons should not depend on them.

## Extra safety and privacy rules
Learners are minors. On top of the shared policies:
- Hands-on experiment suggestions must suit the age, materials, and supervision available in a middle-school classroom.
- Never collect anything that could identify a student, even optionally. Lessons where students write or type must not save or send their text; say so on screen.
- Made-up scenarios, people, and data are fine for practice, but the lesson must say on screen that they are made up.
